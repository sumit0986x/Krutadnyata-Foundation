The Nail-Free Tree Campaign

A hands-on ecological preservation drive that removes heavy commercial iron nails and hoardings embedded in living street trees across Kandivali East, pairs the physical de-nailing work with Angholichi Goli's community outreach, and extends into plantation drives and sustainability education across suburban Mumbai and Thane.

Strategic Scope

Treating environmental responsibility as an active, physical duty

Across suburban Mumbai, countless street trees carry hidden wounds — heavy commercial iron nails hammered in to hang hoardings, banners, and posters, slowly girdling and killing living bark. Krutadnyata Foundation's Nail-Free Tree Campaign confronts this directly: a sustained, hands-on de-nailing drive in Kandivali East, carried out alongside community initiative 'Angholichi Goli', that physically remediates the urban green belt tree by tree.

De-nailing Drive
Kandivali East, Mumbai

Removing heavy iron nails, hoarding wires, and hooks from living trees

Volunteers walk ward by ward through Kandivali East, locating street trees embedded with commercial-grade nails used to pin up advertisements, festival banners, and shop signage. Each nail is carefully extracted by hand, the wound is assessed, and the tree is logged — a slow, deliberate process that has, over repeated drives, de-nailed hundreds of trees and restored a measure of health to the suburb's shrinking green cover.

World Environment Day Plantation

Planting the next generation of trees at Siddharth College, Badlapur

On World Environment Day, the Foundation partnered with educational trusts to run a large-scale tree plantation drive at Siddharth College in Badlapur, Thane. Students and faculty planted native saplings across the campus grounds, pairing the de-nailing work in Mumbai's suburbs with proactive reforestation further out — ensuring the campaign builds green cover forward as much as it repairs damage already done.

Badlapur, Thane

"A tree doesn't need a slogan to survive — it needs the nail taken out. Everything else, the saplings, the seminars, the sustainability talk, is built on that one small, patient act."

Krutadnyata Foundation, Field Note — Kandivali East
